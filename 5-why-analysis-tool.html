What is 5 Why Analysis?

5 Why Analysis is a simple Root Cause Analysis technique used to find the underlying cause of a problem.

The basic idea is to ask "Why?" repeatedly until the real or controllable root cause is identified.

It is called "5 Why" because five levels are commonly used, but you do not always have to stop exactly at five. Sometimes the root cause can be found after three Why questions, while complex problems may require more than five.

How to Use 5 Why Analysis

  1. Clearly define the problem.
  2. Ask why the problem occurred.
  3. Write the evidence-based answer.
  4. Ask why that cause occurred.
  5. Continue until the underlying cause is identified.
  6. Confirm the root cause with actual evidence.
  7. Create corrective and preventive actions.
  8. Verify that the action eliminates recurrence.

5 Why Example - Sheet Metal Quality

Problem: Sheet metal part dimension is out of specification.

Level Why Analysis
Problem Part dimension is out of specification.
Why 1 Bending angle was incorrect.
Why 2 Bending machine setting was incorrect.
Why 3 Machine setting was not verified before production.
Why 4 First-piece inspection was not completed.
Why 5 There was no defined first-piece inspection checklist.
Root Cause Missing standard first-piece inspection checklist and verification process.

Important Points

  • Do not automatically blame the operator.
  • Every Why answer should be supported by facts or evidence whenever possible.
  • The fifth Why is not always the root cause.
  • Stop when you reach a controllable and verified underlying cause.
  • 5 Why identifies a possible root cause, but the cause should be verified before implementing permanent action.
  • For complex problems, combine 5 Why with tools such as Fishbone, Pareto, FMEA or 8D.
