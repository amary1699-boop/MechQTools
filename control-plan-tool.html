What is a Control Plan?

A Control Plan is a structured document used in manufacturing to define how a process and its important characteristics will be controlled and inspected.

Purpose of Control Plan

  • Define process and product characteristics.
  • Define specifications and tolerances.
  • Define measurement methods and gauges.
  • Define inspection sample size and frequency.
  • Define how the process will be controlled.
  • Define what action should be taken when a result is not acceptable.

Important Control Plan Columns

  • Process / Operation: The manufacturing step being controlled.
  • Machine / Equipment: Machine, tool, fixture or equipment used.
  • Characteristic: The product or process feature that must be controlled.
  • Specification / Tolerance: Required drawing or process requirement.
  • Measurement Method: Instrument or method used for inspection.
  • Sample Size: Number of parts or measurements to inspect.
  • Frequency: How often the inspection is performed.
  • Control Method: Method used to keep the process under control.
  • Reaction Plan: Action to take when a result is out of specification.

How to Use Control Plan

  1. Enter basic part and control plan information.
  2. Add one row for each important process operation.
  3. Enter the machine or equipment used.
  4. Enter important product or process characteristics.
  5. Enter the drawing specification or tolerance.
  6. Select the appropriate measurement method or gauge.
  7. Define sample size and inspection frequency.
  8. Define the control method.
  9. Define the reaction plan for an abnormal result.
  10. Generate and print the Control Plan Report.

Control Plan Example

Process

Sheet Metal Bending

Characteristic

Bend Angle

Specification

90° ± 1°

Measurement Method

Digital Angle Gauge

Sample Size

1st piece + 1 piece every 2 hours

Control Method

Verify machine setup and inspect bend angle according to the defined inspection frequency.

Reaction Plan

Stop process, segregate suspect parts, correct machine setting, reinspect affected parts and restart production after verification.

Important Points

  • Control Plan should be based on actual process risks and important product/process characteristics.
  • Specifications should match the applicable drawing, customer requirement or process standard.
  • Measurement equipment should be suitable for the required accuracy and properly controlled.
  • Inspection frequency should be appropriate to the process risk.
  • Reaction plans should clearly define what happens when the process goes out of control or specification.
  • Control Plan should be reviewed and updated when the process, drawing, equipment or control method changes.
  • Control Plan is commonly linked with Process Flow, PFMEA and other quality planning documents.
