Formulas

Gauge R&R:

GRR = √(Repeatability² + Reproducibility²)

% Repeatability:

% Repeatability = (Repeatability ÷ Total Variation) × 100

% Reproducibility:

% Reproducibility = (Reproducibility ÷ Total Variation) × 100

% Gauge R&R:

%GRR = (Gauge R&R ÷ Total Variation) × 100

% Part / Process Variation:

% Part Variation = √(1 − (%GRR ÷ 100)²) × 100

What does Gauge R&R mean?

Repeatability

Repeatability is the variation obtained when the same operator measures the same part using the same gauge repeatedly.

It mainly represents equipment or gauge variation.

Reproducibility

Reproducibility is the variation between different operators measuring the same part.

It can indicate differences caused by operator technique or measurement method.

Gauge R&R

Gauge R&R means Gauge Repeatability and Reproducibility.

It combines repeatability and reproducibility to estimate the variation contributed by the measurement system.

Total Variation

Total variation represents the overall variation considered in the measurement study.

Gauge R&R is compared against this value to understand how much variation comes from the measurement system.

%GRR Interpretation

%GRR General Interpretation
≤ 10% Generally Acceptable
> 10% to 30% May Be Acceptable Depending on Application
> 30% Generally Not Acceptable
Important: These %GRR ranges are general industry guidance. Customer requirements, product risk, measurement resolution, drawing tolerance and the applicable MSA procedure should always be considered.

Example

Suppose:

  • Repeatability = 0.020
  • Reproducibility = 0.015
  • Total Variation = 0.100

Then:

Gauge R&R = √(0.020² + 0.015²) ≈ 0.025

%GRR = (0.025 ÷ 0.100) × 100 ≈ 25%

Therefore, this measurement system falls in the 10%–30% range and may be acceptable depending on the application and customer requirements.

Important: This calculator uses a simplified standard-deviation-based approach for learning and quick engineering estimation. A formal Gauge R&R study may require actual measurement data from multiple parts, operators and trials and may use AIAG MSA procedures such as Average & Range or ANOVA methods. For customer submission or formal MSA approval, follow the required company/customer standard and approved MSA procedure.
