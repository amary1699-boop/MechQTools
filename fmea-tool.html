What is FMEA?

FMEA means Failure Mode and Effects Analysis. It is a systematic method used to identify potential failures in a product, process or system before they create serious problems.

FMEA helps the Quality, Production, Design and Engineering teams prioritize risks and decide where corrective or preventive action is required.

What is Failure Mode?

A Failure Mode is the way in which a product or process could fail to meet the required specification or expectation.

Example: Hole diameter out of specification, wrong bending angle, surface scratch.

Severity (S)

Severity indicates how serious the effect of a failure is. The rating normally ranges from 1 to 10.

  • 1 = Very low effect
  • 5 = Moderate effect
  • 8 = Serious effect
  • 10 = Very severe / critical effect

Occurrence (O)

Occurrence indicates how frequently the potential cause of failure may happen. The rating normally ranges from 1 to 10.

  • 1 = Very unlikely
  • 5 = Occasional
  • 8 = Frequent
  • 10 = Very frequent

Detection (D)

Detection indicates how likely the current controls are to detect the failure or its cause before the product reaches the customer.

  • 1 = Very easy to detect
  • 5 = Moderate detection
  • 8 = Difficult to detect
  • 10 = Almost impossible to detect

Important: In the traditional FMEA scale, a higher Detection number means poorer detection capability.

RPN Formula

RPN means Risk Priority Number. It is calculated using Severity, Occurrence and Detection.

RPN = Severity × Occurrence × Detection

Example: If S = 8, O = 5 and D = 4:

RPN = 8 × 5 × 4 = 160

Risk Level Used in This Tool

  • RPN 1–49: Low Risk
  • RPN 50–99: Medium Risk
  • RPN 100–199: High Risk
  • RPN 200–1000: Very High Risk

These ranges are a practical screening guide. Your company's FMEA procedure or customer-specific requirements may use different risk criteria.

How to Use This FMEA Tool

  • Enter the FMEA process or title.
  • Add the process or operation.
  • Identify the potential failure mode.
  • Describe the effect of the failure.
  • Give Severity from 1 to 10.
  • Identify the potential cause.
  • Give Occurrence from 1 to 10.
  • Enter existing/current controls.
  • Give Detection from 1 to 10.
  • Click Calculate RPN.
  • Review high-risk failure modes.
  • Define recommended corrective actions.

Example FMEA

Process: Sheet Metal Bending

Failure Mode: Bend angle out of specification

Effect: Part does not fit during assembly

Severity: 8

Cause: Incorrect machine setting

Occurrence: 5

Current Control: First-piece inspection

Detection: 4

RPN: 8 × 5 × 4 = 160

This would be classified as High Risk in this tool, so corrective action should be considered.

Important FMEA Points

  • FMEA is a preventive risk analysis method.
  • Do not select ratings randomly.
  • Use actual inspection and process data whenever available.
  • High RPN items should receive priority.
  • A low RPN does not automatically mean zero risk.
  • After corrective action, review the ratings again.
  • Customer-specific FMEA requirements should always be followed.
  • FMEA helps prioritize risk but does not replace engineering judgement.
