What is Fishbone Analysis?

Fishbone Analysis is a structured root-cause analysis technique used to identify and organize possible causes of a problem. It is also known as the Ishikawa Diagram or Cause-and-Effect Diagram.

Why is it called Fishbone?

The traditional diagram looks similar to the skeleton of a fish. The problem is written at the head of the fish and possible causes are arranged along the bones.

What are the 6M categories?

  • Man: People, skill, training and human-related causes.
  • Machine: Equipment, machine, tool, fixture and maintenance causes.
  • Method: Process, procedure, work instruction and sequence causes.
  • Material: Raw material, component, specification and supplier causes.
  • Measurement: Gauge, calibration, measurement method and inspection causes.
  • Environment: Temperature, humidity, lighting, dust and workplace conditions.

How to use Fishbone Analysis?

  1. Clearly define the problem.
  2. Write the problem statement.
  3. Identify possible causes under each 6M category.
  4. Discuss the causes with the concerned team.
  5. Collect evidence for the suspected causes.
  6. Verify the actual root cause.
  7. Implement corrective action.
  8. Take preventive action to avoid recurrence.

Fishbone Analysis Example

Problem

Sheet metal component bend angle is out of specification.

Possible Causes

  • Man: Operator not properly trained.
  • Machine: Press brake setting incorrect.
  • Method: Work instruction does not define setup verification.
  • Material: Material thickness variation.
  • Measurement: Angle measurement method not standardized.
  • Environment: Poor lighting at inspection area.

Verified Root Cause

Machine setup parameters were not verified during first-piece inspection.

Corrective Action

Implement first-piece setup verification and operator confirmation.

Preventive Action

Update work instruction and train all concerned operators.

Important Points

  • Fishbone analysis identifies possible causes; it does not automatically prove the root cause.
  • Root cause should be verified using actual data or evidence.
  • Avoid blaming people without investigating the process.
  • Use 5 Why, data analysis, inspection records and process trials to verify important causes.
  • Corrective action should eliminate the verified root cause.
  • Preventive action should reduce the possibility of recurrence.
