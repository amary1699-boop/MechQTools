What is Bend Deduction?

Bend deduction is the amount subtracted from the sum of the outside dimensions of a bent sheet metal part to obtain the flat pattern length.

Bend Deduction Formula

BD = A + B − Flat Pattern Length

A and B are the outside dimensions of the two legs.

Important Relationship

Bend deduction is closely related to bend allowance and setback. The exact calculation can vary depending on the dimensioning method and sheet metal bending process.

Example

Suppose:

  • Outside dimension A = 100 mm
  • Outside dimension B = 100 mm
  • Material thickness = 2 mm
  • Inside radius = 3 mm
  • Bend allowance = 5.75 mm
For this calculator, the theoretical flat length is:

Flat Length = A + B − Bend Deduction

The calculator uses the standard outside-dimension relationship to determine the bend deduction.

Why is Bend Deduction Important?

Bend deduction is commonly used when developing flat patterns for sheet metal parts. Correct deduction helps the finished part achieve the required dimensions after bending.

Where is it Used?

  • Sheet metal fabrication
  • Press brake operations
  • Laser cutting
  • Flat pattern development
  • CAD sheet metal design
  • Manufacturing drawings

Important Note

Actual bend deduction depends on material, tooling, bending method, inside radius, springback and the way dimensions are specified. For production work, always validate the value against your machine setup or proven shop-floor data.
